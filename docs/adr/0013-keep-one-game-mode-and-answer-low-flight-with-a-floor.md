# Keep one game mode and answer low flight with a floor and a Near Miss

Manual play of Pellegrino Peak (#75) showed the Playful Bump was the least enjoyable thing in the place: nothing about it was fun to meet and nothing was fun to avoid, because a bump was not an encounter with anything but a height test — contact fired below 0.70 of a corridor reaching only 0.86, so every place's low rung and the lower half of its high rung sat inside the bump zone, and flying to a low delight and taking a bump were the same act. The journey will therefore have exactly one game mode: Gentle Help and the Cloud Rest that delivered it leave the MVP, the Playful Bump is confined to a Bump Floor that can never swallow a rung of the Altitude Ladder, and low flight is answered by an earned Near Miss rather than by assistance.

This supersedes ADR-0004's three-bump Cloud Rest clause and ADR-0009's consequence that "Cloud Rest remains a brief consequence after nearby Playful Bumps". It upholds and enforces ADR-0012: "no height is correct and none is inferior" was contradicted by a bump zone covering 78% of reachable height, and becomes a launch-time invariant rather than a sentence.

## Consequences

- There is no adaptive assistance. Gentle Help's easing was never shown to the child and its wider corridor and smaller Stella existed only to catch the bump loop; a child who finds Flight Control hard now gets the same journey as everyone else, and the journey is built to be worth flying at any height.
- Cloud Rest leaves with it. Without Gentle Help to deliver, it was a 1.2-second pause that returned Stella to the height she left and changed nothing. The three-bump rule goes with it: a Playful Bump is a lone wobble that never accumulates. `#51` stories 25 through 28 leave the MVP.
- The Bump Floor is declared as a margin above the lowest reachable height, and the journey refuses to launch if any rung of any place's Altitude Ladder is left with no reachable height clear of the floor — the same refusal ADR-0012 already applies to a rung naming no reachable height. The test is clearance, not non-overlap: every place's low band runs down to the floor of the corridor and the Abbey's settle bell reaches lower still, so a rung that merely reaches into the floor is expected. What is forbidden is a rung the child cannot hold without bumping. A hazard that eats the ladder becomes impossible rather than merely discouraged.
- Golden Bell Abbey's settle bell decides the floor's thickness. It answers heights up to 0.34, the lowest rung in the Edition, so the floor is authored at a margin of 0.04 above the corridor's lowest reachable height rather than the thicker floor first sketched on #75. That leaves contact at 0.24 and the settle bell half its span to be rung in; a 0.12 margin would have left it 0.02 and satisfied the invariant while stranding the bell in practice.
- The Near Miss is promoted from an unearned route milestone to the journey's one reward for flying well. It sounds on the upward crossing out of the band, so it is never announced before the miss is a fact, and it rests between announcements for the reason ADR-0012 gives for the Abbey's bells. It answers in every place, including Rosalia's Rose Garden, whose approved `near-miss-rose-garden.wav` could not previously play because one flag gated both the bump and the near miss.
- Three `cloud-rest-*` masters retire. `playful-bump-rose-garden.wav` remains deliberately unplayed, as the Rose Garden's floor does not bump.
- The Playful Bump was kept on probation because ADR-0005 set out to replace the Original Edition's repeated obstacle pattern and the bump is what remains of it. Manual play at Golden Bell Abbey (#77) settled it: the settle bell can be held clear of the floor on purpose, and the earned Near Miss makes low flight feel like something the child did. The bump stays.
- The Bump Floor stays global and invisible. No place declares where its bumpable things are, because the cheap version of low flight flew well and the expensive one would fix a problem the playtest did not find. Visible bumpable things are unneeded rather than rejected; a later place that wants them opens its own issue and its own ADR, and the launch-time invariant above already guarantees such a floor could not strand a rung.
- The authored values stand as flown: a Bump Floor margin of 0.04, contact at 0.24, a Near Miss band of 0.08 above it, and a Near Miss rest of 1.5 seconds. The rest was raised from the 0.9 the Abbey's bells use because the flight dynamics make the fastest re-crossing of the band about 1.15 seconds, so 0.9 could never fire and would have been dead tuning.
