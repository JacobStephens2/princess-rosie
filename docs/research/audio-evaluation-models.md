# Audio models for Fairytale Soundscape candidate evaluation

Research date: 2026-08-23

## Owner decision after research

On 2026-08-23, the project owner chose paid Gemini 3.7 Flash as the required native-audio evaluator. If it is unavailable or cannot receive the audio, the approved fallback is Muse Spark 1.2 at `xhigh` reasoning effort through the ordinary paid Meta Model API, provided an end-to-end smoke test proves that the request delivers the actual audio. If neither approved evaluator can receive the audio, agents stop and ask rather than substituting another model.

This operational decision supersedes the report's experimental recommendation to compare Gemini with Qwen3.5-Omni-Plus. The owner requires paid model calls to be billed directly by a US-headquartered provider unless an exception is explicitly approved. MiniMax-M3 was also removed from consideration as an audio fallback because its Fireworks endpoint supports text, image, and video rather than native audio input ([Fireworks model page](https://fireworks.ai/models/fireworks/minimax-m3)). The [audio-evaluation policy](../agents/audio-evaluation.md) is authoritative; the rest of this report preserves the evidence and alternatives that informed the decision.

## Bottom line

There is not yet primary-source evidence that any available model is unambiguously better than Gemini 3.7 Flash at the actual task here: choosing among short, gentle, nonverbal game-sound candidates by semantic fit, timbre, fatigue risk, speech contamination, and in-context feel. Gemini 3.7 Flash was released on 2026-08-13, and Google's published evaluation table does not include a sound-understanding benchmark. Its model card confirms audio input, but the published scores cover coding, agents, documents, charts, video, long context, and safety—not short sound effects ([Gemini 3.7 Flash model card](https://deepmind.google/models/model-cards/gemini-3-7-flash/)).

The research-time recommendation was therefore the following calibrated bake-off. It is preserved for context but superseded by the owner decision above:

1. Keep Gemini 3.7 Flash as one blind judge.
2. Test Qwen3.5-Omni-Plus as the strongest easy-to-integrate cross-vendor challenger.
3. If local GPU inference is practical, add MOSS-Audio-8B-Thinking for sound cues and MOSS-Music-8B-Instruct for strongly musical cues.
4. Use Qwen3-Omni-Captioner as an independent, unprompted description and speech/artifact screen, not as the final ranker.
5. Retain deterministic DSP checks and human MacBook/headphone listening for waveform quality, loop seams, spatial presentation, and mastering.

A 20–30 clip calibration set, labeled blind by a human with the project's rubric, is enough to determine whether a challenger adds signal. Compare model agreement with the human labels and retain a second judge only if it improves ranking or catches failures Gemini misses.

## What the evaluator needs to do

The project needs two different kinds of evaluation, and no current model should be treated as authoritative for both.

- **Perceptual/semantic judgment:** Is the cue recognizably a soft petal response, quiet flight texture, restrained magical shimmer, etc.? Is it gentle, nonverbal, non-startling, distinct from its siblings, and unlikely to become tiring? Audio-language models can help here.
- **Signal and mix verification:** Are true peak, loudness, silence, duration, channel layout, stereo correlation, clipping, loop boundary, and in-engine summed level acceptable? These should remain deterministic measurements plus human playback checks. A model's prose about a waveform is not a substitute for measuring the waveform.

The strongest evidence for keeping this split is the preprocessing itself. Gemini combines multichannel input to mono and documents downsampling to a “16 Kbps data resolution” ([Gemini audio-understanding technical details](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/audio#technical-details-about-audio)). Audio Flamingo Next explicitly expects mono 16 kHz input ([AF-Next model card](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/audio-flamingo-next-hf)). MOSS-Audio's public processor configures a 16 kHz mel frontend and takes only the first row of a two-dimensional waveform ([MOSS-Audio processor source](https://github.com/OpenMOSS/MOSS-Audio/blob/main/src/processing_moss_audio.py#L15-L22), [channel handling](https://github.com/OpenMOSS/MOSS-Audio/blob/main/src/processing_moss_audio.py#L206-L223)). OpenAI, Meta, and Alibaba's public API pages do not disclose enough input-channel or resampling behavior to certify mastering fidelity.

## Specialist local gates

Two smaller models can add repeatable evidence without pretending to replace a perceptual judge:

- **LAION-CLAP** embeds text and audio into a shared space, accepts 48 kHz audio in its example, and provides checkpoints for general audio, music, and speech. Use it to compare semantic similarity between each candidate and positive/negative descriptions; it cannot explain a judgment or reliably score gentleness, fatigue, mastering, or loop quality. ([LAION-CLAP repository](https://github.com/LAION-AI/CLAP))
- **YAMNet** predicts 521 AudioSet event classes and expects mono 16 kHz audio. It can flag speech-like classes or obviously wrong events as a local screening signal, but its fixed taxonomy is not a sound-design rubric. ([TensorFlow YAMNet tutorial](https://www.tensorflow.org/hub/tutorials/yamnet))

These gates are especially useful for speech contamination because they are independent of the generative judge's prose. They should trigger review rather than automatic rejection unless calibrated against known-good and known-bad project clips.

## Recommended evaluation stack

### Candidate-set selection

For each three-candidate set:

1. Run deterministic validation first: decodability, duration, channel layout, silence, peak/true peak, loudness, DC offset, clipping indicators, and loop-boundary metrics where relevant.
2. Assign random candidate IDs and omit generator filenames or any text that suggests which candidate should win.
3. Ask Gemini 3.7 Flash and Qwen3.5-Omni-Plus independently for the same JSON fields: semantic fit, nonverbalness, gentleness, startle risk, timbral clarity, fatigue risk, unwanted music/voice, artifacts, distinctness, and ranking. Include an explicit `uncertain` field.
4. For borderline sets, obtain an unprompted Qwen3-Omni-Captioner description. For musical cues, optionally add MOSS-Music; for environmental/movement cues, optionally add MOSS-Audio.
5. Let the implementation agent synthesize the evidence, but require a human MacBook/headphone acceptance pass for final mix-critical cues and any case where judges disagree materially.

Do not average arbitrary 1–10 scores across models as though their scales are calibrated. Prefer ordinal ranking, pairwise preference, explicit failure flags, and agreement with the human-labeled calibration set.

### Speech-contamination screening

Use three independent signals:

1. a dedicated speech/non-speech local classifier such as YAMNet;
2. an unprompted caption or transcription request to Qwen3-Omni-Captioner (or the general judge); and
3. a human check for ambiguous breath, whisper, vocalization, or sung-like material.

This matters because “nonverbal” is broader than “no intelligible transcript”: whisper-like phonemes, sighs, distant voices, chants, and sung tones may violate the project even if ASR returns an empty string.

### What no model should approve

Keep the following outside model authority:

- stereo width, localization, phase/correlation, and mono compatibility;
- true peak and loudness targets;
- clipping, DC offset, silence, or exact duration;
- sample-accurate or spectrally smooth loop boundaries;
- summed in-engine headroom and ducking;
- translation to MacBook built-in speakers and headphone fatigue.

Models may flag a suspicious click, harsh transient, or apparent clipping, but the build pipeline should decide mechanical pass/fail from the source waveform, and a human should decide final translation in the actual mix.

## Decision

The research did not establish an unambiguously superior evaluator for this exact short-SFX task. After considering the evidence and provider constraint, the project owner selected **paid Gemini 3.7 Flash** as the required native-audio evaluator and **paid Muse Spark 1.2 at `xhigh`** through the Meta Model API as its failure fallback. Muse must first pass an end-to-end native-audio smoke test; Muse Code and the Contributor tier are not approved delivery paths.

Qwen3.5-Omni-Plus, Qwen3-Omni-Captioner, MiniMax-M3, MOSS-Audio, MOSS-Music, Audio Flamingo Next, and OpenAI gpt-audio-1.5 are not operational substitutes under the current policy. Preserve their comparison above as research context rather than instructions. Continue to use deterministic waveform checks and human MacBook/headphone listening alongside the approved evaluator, as specified in the [audio-evaluation policy](../agents/audio-evaluation.md).
